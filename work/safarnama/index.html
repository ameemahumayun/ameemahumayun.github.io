Problem statement: the disconnect in domestic tourism

The tourism industry favours the travellers' experience, keeping them confined to pre-packaged tours and commercialized attractions with little space for meaningful local engagement.

Locals, though rich in cultural knowledge and skills, often lack structured, financially viable ways to share them.

Few attempts exist to mitigate this. Digitized spaces fall short as they aren't designed to improve the tourist–local relationship, while non-digitized solutions remain informal, inconsistent, and constrained by poor infrastructure.

Design journey

  • Survey — 80 responses
  • Interviews — 21 participants
  • Solution design
  • Testing — 26 participants

Quantitative research: the main findings

Key attractions for travelers

  • Most popular: culture, food, thrill, fun, and social experiences combined (15.6%)
  • Others: fun alone (9.1%), social experiences alone (5.2%)

Information sources

  • Social media alone: 15.6%
  • Social media & travel blogs combined: 15.6%
  • Google & social media combined: 11.7%
  • Locals exclusively: 7.8% · travel blogs alone: 7.8%

Willingness to engage

  • Positive: ~49% · negative: ~31% · conditional or hesitant: ~20%

Experience with local help

  • Neutral / no significant interaction: ~56.2% · positive: ~43.8%

Issues faced by travelers

  • No significant issues: around 25%
  • Issues mentioned: network problems, poor sanitation (washrooms), misguidance through Google Maps

Qualitative research: the main findings

Opportunities and needs

  • Most contact begins via businesses (shops, transport, guesthouses)
  • Locals say strong community networks are essential for sustainable tourism
  • Economic opportunity: locals building homestays, food stalls, tour services
  • Economic concern: non-local, capitalistic organizations trying to monopolize growing tourist markets

Digital and non-digital infrastructure

  • Poor internet, low literacy, cash systems, and reliance on word-of-mouth hinder coordination
  • Yet locals extensively use TikTok, Instagram, YouTube; prefer audio/video content over text
  • Safety and transparency: demand for clear roads, emergency support, fair pricing
  • Reliable information: tourists want pre-trip insights; locals want visibility

Tourism as a source of cultural tension

  • Cultural norms like parda, Muharram, respect for private land, or gendered space are often overlooked
  • Privacy violations like photography without consent fuel resentment
  • Locals fear cultural deterioration similar to Murree, as younger generations absorb outsider behaviors and the area is commercialized beyond recognition

Trust in tourist-local interactions

  • Tourists report price hikes, last-minute changes, and unclear service expectations
  • Locals, in turn, face distrust or accusations when payments are disputed
  • The absence of structured systems creates friction
  • Yet tourists and locals report helping each other in times of need (natural disasters, being lost, etc.)

Findings to features

  • Sign up as different roles
  • Enjoy different privileges
  • Connect with everyone directly

Main features

1. Sign-up flow

Sign-up is through phone number and CNIC pictures. The app auto-fills information by parsing the picture, and sign-in/up runs through OTP. This accounts for differing literacy and ensures security.

2. Roles

Users sign up as either traveler or host — or both. Both personas have different privileges. As a traveler, register as a Travel Buddy; as a local, sign up as a Tour Guide. Users can switch roles from the settings menu.

3. Explore

As a traveler, explore spots, activities, and food; see reviews for each service individually. Use the map to see crowdsourced information. As a local, provide these services and see your map reviews get precedence.

4. Others

Other relevant features like SOS calls, emergency protocol PDFs, and a general "Other Services" listing where locals can freelance any service they wish.

Testing research: the main findings

Mismatch between labels & mental models

"Explore" misread as Home; profile/settings split confused users. Users followed familiar logic that wasn't supported in the prototype. Icons, labels, and layout must align with user expectations.

Guidance gaps & language confusion

Local, Host; Traveller, Guest — these were not interchangeable for users. A clear onboarding tutorial and help screen are essential. One-line explanations often resolved confusion.

Polarized language & layout feedback

Terms like "Buddy Up" had opposing reactions; visual consistency was praised by some, criticized by others. These require further testing.

What worked

The SOS feature was universally praised. Users liked the concept and found the design practical despite prototype flaws. Several said they'd use the app in real life, and users liked the consistency between the two profiles, which they said was "easier."
