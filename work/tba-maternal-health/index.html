TBA Maternal-Health Prototype

A low-literacy maternal-health prototype for Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs), built in collaboration with Aga Khan University and VITAL Pakistan — designed to hold clinical guidance in a form frontline attendants can actually use in the field.

Launch Prototype

Context

Traditional Birth Attendants remain a real point of contact for maternal care in many low-resource communities in Pakistan, often working with limited formal training materials and inconsistent access to updated clinical guidance. The question behind this prototype was simple to state and hard to solve: what does maternal-health guidance look like when it has to be usable by someone with limited literacy, under real time pressure, without a clinician standing next to them?

Existing training material — official TBA curricula and WHO guidance documents — is written for classroom instruction, not point-of-care reference. The prototype's job was to translate that material into something a TBA could actually reach for during or immediately after a delivery.

Approach

I synthesized official TBA curricula, WHO guidance, and focus-group findings from frontline TBAs into the prototype's information architecture, content structure, and interaction flows. Each source did different work: the curricula and WHO guidance anchored clinical correctness, while the focus groups surfaced how TBAs actually think through a case — what they check first, what they're unsure about, and when they know to refer a patient onward.

The content structure was built around that real decision order rather than the order guidance documents happen to present material in, so the prototype follows how a TBA would actually reason through a situation rather than how a training manual is organized.

Design considerations

The design was iteratively refined around four constraints that shaped nearly every decision:

  • Information overload — clinical guidance is dense by nature. The prototype had to surface only what's decision-relevant in the moment, deferring detail rather than presenting it all at once.
  • Literacy constraints — content had to work for readers with limited literacy, favoring visual and structural cues over dense text, without stripping out clinical precision.
  • Usability under pressure — a TBA using this mid-case is not browsing; she needs to find the right guidance fast. Navigation and hierarchy were built around speed of retrieval.
  • Referral needs — knowing when a case exceeds a TBA's scope is as important as the guidance itself, so referral triggers are built into the flow rather than left as a separate reference.
