TBA Helper

A low-literacy maternal-health prototype for Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs), built in collaboration with Aga Khan University and VITAL Pakistan — designed to hold clinical guidance in a form frontline attendants can actually use in the field.

Launch Prototype

Context

  • TBAs are a real point of contact for maternal care in many low-resource Pakistani communities, often without updated clinical guidance on hand.
  • The core question: what does maternal-health guidance look like when it has to work for someone with limited literacy, under time pressure, with no clinician nearby?
  • Official TBA curricula and WHO guidance are written for classroom instruction, not point-of-care reference — the prototype had to translate that into something usable in the moment.

Approach

  • Synthesized official TBA curricula, WHO guidance, and focus-group findings from frontline TBAs into the information architecture, content structure, and interaction flows.
  • Curricula and WHO guidance anchored clinical correctness; the focus groups surfaced how TBAs actually reason through a case — what they check first, what they're unsure about, when they refer onward.
  • Content structure follows that real decision order, not the order training manuals happen to present material in.

Design considerations

  • Information overload — clinical guidance is dense by nature. The prototype had to surface only what's decision-relevant in the moment, deferring detail rather than presenting it all at once.
  • Literacy constraints — content had to work for readers with limited literacy, favoring visual and structural cues over dense text, without stripping out clinical precision.
  • Usability under pressure — a TBA using this mid-case is not browsing; she needs to find the right guidance fast. Navigation and hierarchy were built around speed of retrieval.
  • Referral needs — knowing when a case exceeds a TBA's scope is as important as the guidance itself, so referral triggers are built into the flow rather than left as a separate reference.
